Same average, different sleeper

A note from HackedSelf

Two sleepers can bank identical weekly hours and still be different organisms. The tracker prints one number, the weekly average, and the average deletes the schedule underneath it. Alertness, hormones, and body temperature key off when sleep happens as much as how much of it happens.

Put two weeks side by side. Sleeper A is in bed 11:00 pm to 6:15 am all seven nights: 7.25 x 7 = 50.75 hours. Sleeper B runs 12:00 to 6:15 on weeknights and 1:00 to 10:45 am on the weekend: 6.25 x 5 = 31.25, plus 9.75 x 2 = 19.5, and 31.25 + 19.5 = the same 50.75. One weekly total, two different sleeps.

The difference lives in mid-sleep, the midpoint of each night. A's midpoint sits at 2:37 am every night of the week. B's walks from 3:07 am on Wednesday to 5:52 am on Saturday, a 2 hour 45 minute slide, delivered twice a week. The circadian clock re-phases roughly an hour a day, so every weekend B spends about three days dragging the clock back, and Monday starts the drag over. That is social jet lag with no boarding pass, a round trip three time zones west, 52 weekends a year, taken without ever leaving the bedroom.

Run the week like a protocol and the compliance numbers land differently. Call a night a kept dose when bedtime lands within 30 minutes of target. A keeps 7 of 7. B keeps 5 of 7, 71 percent adherence. A supplement taken five nights out of seven gets flagged at the next checkup; the same adherence in sleep timing gets called basically fine because the weekly total still reads seven and a quarter hours.

None of this costs anything to fix, which is the strange part. A $300 sleep tracker amortized nightly is $0.82 per reading, spent measuring variance it cannot remove. The intervention is a $0 alarm: one for getting into bed, which almost nobody sets, alongside the one for getting up. Average hours answer how much; consistency answers how well, and the second number is free.
